# Expandable Spinal Implant for Minimally Invasive Surgery

**McGill University | SuPER Lab | Mechanical Engineering Capstone**

**Mechanical Design • Medical Devices • FEA • Additive Manufacturing**

## Project Overview

This project focused on the design and development of an expandable vertebral body replacement implant for minimally invasive spinal surgery.

Traditional vertebral body replacement procedures can require extensive surgical access to accommodate the implant. Our objective was to develop a compact implant capable of being delivered through a minimally invasive approach and expanded after placement to provide structural support.

The project was developed in collaboration with the Surgical Performance Enhancement and Robotics (SuPER) Lab at the Montreal General Hospital, incorporating feedback from practicing surgeons throughout the design process.

## Design and Development

The implant was developed through an iterative engineering design process, balancing mechanical performance, surgical accessibility, and manufacturability.

Key design considerations included:

- **Expandable mechanism:** Developed a compact implant with an angled actuation mechanism to enable expansion after insertion.
- **Structural support:** Designed the implant to withstand spinal loading conditions while providing stable contact with adjacent vertebrae.
- **Fixation and stability:** Incorporated fixation features to improve the implant's mechanical stability.
- **Modularity:** Explored a modular configuration to accommodate varying anatomical requirements.
- **Surgical integration:** Refined the design based on feedback from surgeons and considerations specific to minimally invasive procedures.

## Engineering Analysis and Prototyping

### Finite Element Analysis

ANSYS simulations were used to evaluate the implant's structural response under loading conditions informed by clinical requirements.

The analysis supported the evaluation of stress distributions, deformation, and design tradeoffs throughout development.

### Prototyping and Manufacturing

Multiple iterations were produced using resin, PLA, and TPU to evaluate mechanical concepts, assembly, and functionality.

The final prototype was manufactured in titanium using powder bed fusion additive manufacturing.

## Project Outcome

The project resulted in a titanium prototype of an expandable vertebral body replacement implant, supported by engineering drawings, structural analysis, and iterative mechanical design.

Our team received the **Best Capstone Project Award** for our engineering cohort.

The experience reinforced the importance of combining mechanical engineering with clinical input when developing medical devices.
